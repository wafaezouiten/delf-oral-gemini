# Gemini Live against the incumbent: what the reproduction actually shows

The deliverable this whole reproduction exists to produce (#12): the same DELF B2
sittings graded by both systems, compared criterion by criterion, with cost and
latency measured rather than asserted.

Everything below rests on two recorded runs, both in `docs/comparison/runs/`:

| Run | What | Command |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `2026-08-16T09-49-57-032Z.json` | 6 sittings × 2 repeats, both correctors | `pnpm compare -- --repeats=2` |
| `live-2026-08-16T09-57-43-573Z.json` | a real Gemini Live débat, 4 turns | `pnpm measure:live -- --turns=4` |

Both are re-runnable. `pnpm compare:summarise` regenerates every table in
§[Criterion by criterion](#criterion-by-criterion) from the run file, so no number
here is transcribed by hand.

---

## 1. The unit problem, which is also the first finding

The two systems do not produce the same kind of object, and no amount of harness
design makes them.

**This system** corrects a *sitting*: the monologue suivi and the débat are one
épreuve, one transcript and one mark out of 25.

**The incumbent** corrects a *tâche*. Its simulator practises one part at a time,
`evaluateOralPerformance` takes a single `tache` number, and each correction
carries its own /25. A candidate over there sits a full B2 oral as two separate
runs and receives two separate marks that are never combined.

So each sitting was put through each system in that system's own unit — one
correction here, two over there — and the incumbent's ten criterion values per
sitting were folded onto this system's five using **this system's own attribution
rule** (`evaluate.ts`, `TASK_ATTRIBUTION`): réalisation de la tâche is read from
the monologue correction, interaction from the débat correction, and the three
linguistic criteria are shown from both because both are legitimate.

That fold is deliberately generous to the incumbent — it hands each pragmatic
criterion the half of the sitting where it is most likely to be earned. The
alternative, averaging the two corrections, would invent bands the grille has no
value for: the mean of 1 and 5 is 3, a legal-looking number nobody awarded.

**Why this is a finding and not bookkeeping.** Look at « interaction » in the
incumbent's tâche-1 column below. On `b2-solide`, on `morphosyntaxe-faible` and on
`lexique-pauvre` it is **1/5** — because it is grading interaction in a monologue,
where by construction there is none. The incumbent's own product never surfaces
that number next to a débat, so nobody notices. Fold the two corrections together
and it becomes visible immediately: a fifth of the incumbent's grille is being
scored against a task that does not contain the thing the criterion measures.

### The corpus

Six whole B2 sittings, written for this comparison
(`scripts/comparison/sittings.ts`), keeping the incumbent's own fixture design —
sabotage exactly one criterion, make the other four conspicuously strong, so a
corrector forming a single global impression is distinguishable from one applying
the grille.

| Sitting | Designed weakness |
| --- | --- |
| `b2-solide` | none — the control |
| `morphosyntaxe-faible` | strong argument, agrammatical French |
| `interaction-faible` | excellent monologue, folds completely under contradiction |
| `lexique-pauvre` | ideas and structure fine, vocabulary systematically poor |
| `hors-sujet` | rich fluent French that never takes a position |
| `candidat-quasi-muet` | barely speaks; both parts under their duration floor |

The incumbent's existing ten-fixture suite (`delf-preps/src/lib/orale-eval/fixtures.ts`)
could not be reused, and the reason is the unit problem again: each of its
fixtures is one tâche, so feeding one here means presenting a sitting whose other
half is empty — which this rubric docks 3 points for and whose « interaction »
this prompt says is judged on a débat that is not there. Every fixture would have
diverged for reasons about the harness rather than about either corrector.

## 3. Divergences

Each one is either an expected consequence of a documented departure, a finding
about the incumbent, or a defect — and the defect is filed.

### 3.1 Phonologie, ours +2 across the board — **defect, filed as [#29](https://github.com/wafaezouiten/delf-oral-gemini/issues/29)**

The largest and most systematic divergence: +2.0, +2.5, +2.5, +2.5 on four of six
sittings. Both correctors were reading text.

| | `phonologieNonEvaluable: true` | Bands awarded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| This system | 2 / 11 | 5 on seven of eleven |
| Incumbent | **24 / 24** | never above 3 |

The incumbent declared phonologie unassessable on **every single correction** and
capped itself at 3. This system awarded 5/5 seven times — including to
`morphosyntaxe-faible`, whose French is conspicuously agrammatical — justifying it,
verbatim, as « aucune hésitation, aucun faux départ ».

Part of this is the audit's deliberate departure: `src/lib/delf/criteria.ts` drops
the incumbent's "PHONOLOGIE À PARTIR D'UN TEXTE" prudence bullet, because a project
whose justification is real audio should not ship an instruction that exists to
compensate for having none. That decision stands.

What the audit did not decide is what the **fallback** does when the audio is
missing, and the replacement (`phonology-instruction.ts`, `TEXT_INSTRUCTION`) is
*more* confident than the thing it replaced — in the one mode with strictly less
evidence. `finish/route.ts` selects text mode whenever both recordings fail to
upload, so this is reachable in production and inflates a stored, candidate-visible
mark by up to 2 bands. Filed.

### 3.2 « Interaction » scored on a monologue — **structural departure, working as intended**

The incumbent's tâche-1 correction returns interaction **1/5** on `b2-solide`,
`morphosyntaxe-faible` and `lexique-pauvre`, and **5/5** on `interaction-faible` —
the one sitting whose candidate actually collapses under contradiction. Read as a
judgement it is backwards; read correctly it is not a judgement at all, because
there is no interaction in a monologue to judge.

This system's `TASK_ATTRIBUTION` exists precisely to prevent it, and is documented
in `evaluate.ts` as this project's addition rather than a ported rule. It is the
clearest evidence in the run that the one-sitting-one-mark shape is worth what it
costs.

### 3.3 `interaction-faible`: the profile the incumbent structurally cannot see — **the headline structural result**

This system: **17/25**, with interaction 1/5 and réalisation de la tâche 5/5. One
mark, and it says exactly what happened — a candidate who prepared a superb
exposé and could not defend a word of it.

The incumbent: **19/25** for the monologue and **7–11/25** for the débat, as two
separate results, on two separate screens, that are never reconciled. Neither
number describes the candidate. The first says "excellent", the second says
"failing", and the fact that they are the same person in the same épreuve is
information the incumbent's data model cannot hold.

This is the single strongest argument in the comparison for the reproduction's
central design choice, and it is not a scoring difference — both systems graded
each half correctly.

### 3.4 The near-silent candidate: 0/25 here, 2/25 there — **finding about the incumbent**

`EVALUATION_RULES` — ported verbatim, identical text in both systems — contains:

> CAS LIMITE : si aucune réplique « CANDIDAT: » ne contient de contenu évaluable,
> mettez 0 à TOUS les critères.

This system applied it: 0 on all five criteria, both runs. The incumbent awarded
1 on all five, both runs, both tâches — its own justification conceding « la tâche
n'est pas réellement réalisée ». Same rule, same words, different model, different
compliance.

The consequence is not cosmetic. The incumbent's raw 5/25 **clears** the DELF
competency floor before the duration penalty drops it to 2/25; this system's 0/25
is below the floor at every step. Nothing here can be filed against this
repository — this is a divergence in the incumbent's favour that this system does
not share.

### 3.5 Run-to-run instability, entirely the incumbent's — **explained, and consequential**

Repeating every correction twice:

| | Criterion readings compared | Moved between runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| This system | 25 (5 sittings × 5 criteria) | **0** |
| Incumbent | 60 (6 sittings × 2 tâches × 5) | **11 (18%)** |

Every one of the eleven unstable readings is the incumbent's. Its /25 swung by up
to 6 points on identical input — `hors-sujet` tâche 2 scored 15 then 9,
`morphosyntaxe-faible` tâche 2 scored 19 then 15.

The cause is in the code, not the model: `evaluate.ts` pins `temperature: 0.2`
with a comment saying why ("grading must be as reproducible as the model allows");
`evaluateOralPerformance` passes no temperature at all and inherits the provider
default. A candidate re-submitting the same épreuve to the incumbent can lose six
marks to sampling.

Two honest caveats. Two repeats establish that the incumbent is unstable; they do
**not** establish that this system is stable — zero movement in 25 readings is
consistent with a much lower non-zero rate. And a low temperature buys
reproducibility, not correctness: a corrector can be perfectly repeatable and
repeatably wrong.

### 3.6 Leniency at the top of the scale — **open, not filed**

`b2-solide` scored a clean **25/25** here, twice, against the incumbent's 17–19
and 23. Roughly 2 of those points are §3.1's phonologie. The rest is the
`SCALE_USE` instruction, which is also this project's addition and exists for a
measured reason (`docs/EVALUATION_FINDINGS.md`: without it the corrector put 3 on
everything). It may simply be tuned a notch generous.

Not filed, because this corpus cannot settle it: `b2-solide` was *written* to be
a strong performance, and no sitting in it is designed to land at a uniform 3. See
§7.

## 4. Cost per sitting

### The correction

| Correction | Calls | Median latency | Slowest | Mean input tok | Mean output tok | Cost per sitting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| This system (1 call/sitting) | 11 | 15.8s | 24.4s | 2 006 | 2 331 | **$0.0102** |
| Incumbent (2 calls/sitting) | 24 | 12.8s | 15.0s | 1 913 | 1 022 | **$0.0402** |

Token counts are measured. Prices are quoted from published rate cards
(`scripts/comparison/pricing.ts`), read 2026-08-16.

Two things had to be got right for this row to mean anything:

- **Reasoning tokens are billed and invisible.** This system's corrector spends
  nearly three times more output on thinking than on answering: across the eleven
  corrections, a mean of **634 tokens of JSON behind 1 697 tokens of reasoning**.
  Costing it from prompt + response alone understates it by 73%, which is why
  `EvaluationUsage.thoughtsTokens` exists. The figure above uses response +
  reasoning.
- **The incumbent's `outputTokens` already includes its reasoning** (AI SDK 7
  reports total output, with `outputTokenDetails.reasoningTokens` as a breakdown),
  so the two columns are comparable.

This system does **four times more reasoning per sitting and still costs a quarter
as much**, because the rate difference (a flash model at $0.75/$3.75 against
$2.50/$15.00) is larger than the token difference.

### The conversation, which is the half that matters

A correction is seconds. A sitting is twenty minutes of live audio, and that is
where the money is. Measured against the real service — a genuine Live débat
session, four turns, 157.6 s (`pnpm measure:live`):

| | Measured | Per 20-min sitting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| This system — Gemini Live | 3 960 prompt + 524 response tokens over 157.6 s → 1 507 and 199 tok/min → **$0.0069/min** | **≈ $0.14** (extrapolated) |
| Incumbent — ElevenLabs agent | *not measured* | **≈ $2.00** at the published $0.10/min |

Read the qualifiers, because they run in opposite directions:

- This system's per-minute figure is an **upper bound**: every prompt token was
  priced at the audio rate, including the text ones. The session's last usage
  snapshot attributes 2 422 prompt tokens to audio and 1 307 to text, and text on
  a Live session is billed lower — so the real figure is under $0.0069/min.
- The extrapolation to 20 minutes assumes the measured rate holds. The measurement
  supports this — context growth was linear (+889, +779, +918 tokens per turn),
  not quadratic, because `contextWindowCompression: { slidingWindow: {} }` caps
  what is re-billed. Without that config the number would compound.
- The synthetic candidate is more fluent than a real B2 learner and drew shorter
  examiner replies, so a real sitting costs somewhat more.
- **The incumbent's conversation cost is quoted, not measured.** There is no
  ElevenLabs key in the reference checkout's environment, and its `NEXT_PUBLIC_AGENT_ID`
  points at a live production agent that should not be dialled for a benchmark.

### Together

| | Conversation | Correction | Total per sitting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| This system | ≈ $0.14 | $0.0102 | **≈ $0.15** |
| Incumbent | ≈ $2.00 | $0.0402 | **≈ $2.04** |

Roughly **13× cheaper per sitting**, and the ratio is driven almost entirely by
the conversation, not the grading. The single largest caveat in this report sits
on the largest number in it: $2.00 of the incumbent's $2.04 is a rate card.

## 5. Latency

Per correction the incumbent is *faster* — 12.8 s median against 15.8 s — which is
what more reasoning buys. Per sitting it is slower, because a sitting there is two
corrections: **25.5 s against 15.8 s**.

Neither is a product problem. Both are well inside what a candidate will tolerate
on a results screen after a fifty-minute épreuve, and the difference would not
change a decision.

## 6. Failure modes, and what the candidate sees

| System | Calls | Failures | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| This system | 12 | 1 | **8.3%** |
| Incumbent | 24 | 0 | 0% |

The one failure was a Gemini **503, "This model is currently experiencing high
demand"**, after 5.3 s — a free-tier capacity refusal, not a bug. It is the
dominant operational risk this comparison found, and one run of twelve calls
cannot pin its real rate; the honest reading is "non-trivial and load-dependent",
and a billed key changes the picture.

What the candidate sees is already correct, and was verified against the code path
rather than assumed:

1. `evaluateExam` throws rather than fabricating a grade — deliberate; a made-up
   mark is worse than a visible failure.
2. `finish/route.ts` catches it and answers **502** with « L'évaluation a échoué.
   Réessayez dans un instant. »
3. `describeFinishFailure` classifies every 5xx as retryable, so the candidate is
   shown the route's own sentence and a working « Réessayer l'envoi » button.
4. The recording stays in the tab. Nothing is lost, and the retry regrades.

The incumbent's own resilience is not free either — it carries `maxRetries: 3`
inside `generateText`, so an unknown number of transient failures were absorbed
silently before its 0% surfaced. The two figures are not measured the same way.

## 7. Limitations

Stated plainly, because several of them bound the conclusions above.

1. **The corpus is written, not recorded.** Six hand-authored transcripts. No real
   candidate, no real accent, no ASR errors. Written French is cleaner than any
   transcript of speech, which is one reason §3.1's phonologie result is as stark
   as it is.
2. **Both correctors were run on text.** The audio phonology path — the audit's
   deliberate departure and this project's central claim — **is not measured
   here**. It cannot be: there is no recording of a candidate who does not exist,
   and synthesising one would measure a TTS engine's accent.
   `docs/AUDIO_EVALUATION.md` says the audio-versus-text experiment has still not
   been run on real learner speech. **Until it is, the reproduction's headline
   justification is argued rather than demonstrated.** That is the largest gap in
   this report.
3. **Two repeats.** Enough to prove the incumbent unstable; not enough to prove
   this system stable, nor to separate a genuine 1-band divergence from sampling.
4. **`lexique-pauvre` has n=1 on this side** — its first correction was the 503.
5. **The corpus has no mid-band sitting.** Marks here cluster at 0, 15, 17, 17, 23,
   25 by design. §3.6's leniency question needs a sitting written to be uniformly
   *adequate*, and there isn't one.
6. **Prices are quoted, and one whole leg is unmeasured.** The ElevenLabs $2.00 is
   a rate card. Vercel's AI Gateway is a reseller, so the incumbent's correction
   cost is a floor rather than its invoice. Gemini's rate is introductory and
   posted to double on 2027-01-01, which alone halves the correction-cost gap.
7. **One model each.** `gemini-3.6-flash` here (the strongest a free key reaches)
   against `openai/gpt-5.4` there. Several divergences may be model differences
   rather than design differences; nothing here separates the two.
8. **The incumbent ran with its default prompt.** Its template is admin-editable
   and production may be running a tuned version; `DEFAULT_ORAL_EVAL_TEMPLATE` is
   what was measured.

## 8. Recommendation

**Adopt the Gemini Live implementation for B2 production orale, conditionally.**
The evidence supports it on three grounds, in descending order of confidence:

1. **The sitting is the right unit** (§3.3). `interaction-faible` is a real,
   common candidate profile that the incumbent's two-corrections-per-épreuve shape
   structurally cannot represent — it reports the same candidate as excellent and
   as failing, and never reconciles them. This is a data-model result, not a
   scoring one, and it does not depend on which model grades.
2. **Reproducibility** (§3.5). Zero movement in 25 criterion readings against the
   incumbent's 18%, and the cause is a one-line difference anyone can inspect. A
   grade that moves 6 points on resubmission is hard to defend to a candidate.
3. **Cost** (§4). Roughly 13× cheaper per sitting, dominated by the conversation
   leg — though $2.00 of the incumbent's $2.04 is quoted, not measured.

### The conditions

These are conditions, not caveats. The recommendation is weaker than it looks
until they are met:

- **[#29](https://github.com/wafaezouiten/delf-oral-gemini/issues/29) is fixed
  before any candidate is graded.** Text mode currently awards up to 5/25 of a
  candidate-visible mark on no acoustic evidence, and it is reached by nothing
  more exotic than a failed upload.
- **Run the audio-versus-text experiment on real learner speech**
  (`docs/AUDIO_EVALUATION.md`, still unrun). The audio phonology claim is the
  reproduction's principal justification and this report does not test it. If it
  does not hold, the case above narrows to §3.3 and §4 — still a case, but a
  different one.
- **Grade on a billed key.** The 503 in §6 is a free-tier capacity refusal, and
  8.3% is not an acceptable failure rate for a candidate who has just spent fifty
  minutes on an épreuve, however well the retry is handled.
- **Re-run this comparison with more repeats and a mid-band sitting** before
  treating any single-band divergence in §2 as real, and to settle §3.6.
- **Measure the ElevenLabs leg, or stop quoting a 13×.** One recorded production
  conversation would convert the largest number in this report from a rate card
  into evidence.

### What this report does not claim

That either corrector is *right*. There is no human DELF examiner's mark anywhere
in this comparison — the two systems are measured against each other and against
the designed weakness of each sitting, never against a jury. Agreement between
them is not accuracy, and this reproduction has never had a ground truth.
